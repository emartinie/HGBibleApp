# Week 1 — In the Beginning
## Made by God, called into His light

*HomeGroups devotional pilot · Seven daily reflections*

**Weekly readings:** Genesis 1:1–6:8; Isaiah 42:5–43:10; John 1:1–14.  
**Companion passage for the weekly closing:** Revelation 21:1–5.

Bereshit, the Hebrew name for Genesis, means “in the beginning.” This week begins with God creating a good world and giving human beings a place within it. It also tells the truth about our distrust, violence, and broken relationships. Isaiah speaks of the Creator bringing light and calling His people by name. John introduces Jesus, the Messiah—whose Hebrew name is Yeshua—as the eternal Word through whom all things were made, now dwelling among us.

These readings invite a response: receive your life as a gift, trust God's wisdom, take responsibility for your choices, and walk toward His light.

Read one reflection each day, alongside the week's fuller Bible study. The passages below are shorter focus readings, not a replacement for the complete weekly readings. Use the Bible translation you normally read.

## Day 1 — Begin with God

**Read:** Genesis 1:1–5; John 1:1–5  
**Consider:** Where do I need to listen before I act?

We often begin with ourselves: what we need, what we fear, what we intend to accomplish. Genesis begins with God. Before anyone builds a city, makes a mistake, or offers an explanation, God is already there.

The opening scene contains darkness and an earth not yet ordered for human life. God speaks, and light appears. Creation depends on His initiative. That does not promise that every confusing circumstance in our lives will resolve immediately. It does remind us that our circumstances are not the measure of God's ability to act.

John deliberately returns us to the beginning. The Word is with God and is God; all things come into being through Him. The light that enters our world in Jesus belongs to the One through whom the world was made.

Beginning with God changes how we enter this week. We can pause before reacting. We can ask what He has already said before demanding a new answer. We can acknowledge that we need His light, even when we would prefer to appear certain.

You do not have to understand the whole week before taking today's faithful step.

**Practice:** Before your first demanding task, pause and read these passages slowly. Name one decision in which you need God's wisdom. Choose one response consistent with what Scripture already makes clear.

**Pray:** Creator God, help me begin with You. Bring Your light to my thinking, and teach me to listen before I act. Lead me in the way of Jesus. Amen.

## Day 2 — Carry His Image

**Read:** Genesis 1:26–31  
**Consider:** Whose dignity am I finding difficult to recognize?

Human beings enter the creation story with a remarkable description: they are made in God's image. Male and female receive this dignity together. It precedes achievement, wealth, influence, and anyone else's approval.

The passage also gives humanity responsibility within creation. Bearing God's image is a calling to represent Him in the world He has made. We are entrusted with life that belongs to Him.

This reaches into ordinary relationships. The person whose opinion frustrates you bears God's image. So does the person whose needs interrupt your plans. So do you, on a day when you accomplish little and struggle to see your own worth.

Sin can distort our conduct terribly; acknowledging human dignity does not excuse harm or remove the need for boundaries. It gives us a reason to resist humiliation, exploitation, and contempt. We can confront wrongdoing without forgetting that the person involved is human.

Following Jesus means learning to use our strength for another person's good. We begin close to home: with our words, our attention, and the way we treat people who cannot offer us anything in return.

**Practice:** Give one person your undivided attention today. If you need to address a disagreement, describe the behavior honestly while speaking with respect.

**Pray:** Father, teach me to honor the people You have made. Help me receive my own life with gratitude and use my words and abilities to care for others. Amen.

## Day 3 — Receive the Gift of Rest

**Read:** Genesis 2:1–3, 15–17  
**Consider:** What does my use of time reveal about whom I trust?

The creation account reaches its seventh day with God resting, blessing the day, and making it holy. Rest belongs within the rhythm of a good creation.

Genesis also places the human being in the garden to cultivate and care for it. Work has purpose. Alongside that work, God establishes a boundary concerning the tree of the knowledge of good and evil. Human freedom is lived in relationship with the Creator, whose wisdom we are called to trust.

Rest and limits can expose the same struggle. We want to keep control: one more task, one more purchase, one more attempt to secure the future. Sometimes responsibilities genuinely require our attention. Sometimes we keep moving because stillness makes us feel vulnerable.

The seventh day introduces the biblical foundation of Sabbath—a time set apart for rest. Christians approach its observance in differing ways. The creation account gives all of us something to consider: time can be received from God, and our lives need room to acknowledge Him.

Rest will not finish every task for us. It can help us remember that our worth and the world's future do not depend entirely on our output.

**Practice:** Set aside a realistic period to stop ordinary work and distractions. Offer that time to God through quiet, prayer, or grateful attention to His creation.

**Pray:** Lord, help me work faithfully and rest trustfully. Teach me to respect Your wisdom and receive time with gratitude. Amen.

## Day 4 — Come Out of Hiding

**Read:** Genesis 3:1–13, 21  
**Consider:** What am I hiding behind excuses or blame?

The temptation in the garden begins by questioning God's instruction and goodness. The forbidden fruit becomes desirable, and the human beings reach for it. Afterward, they hide.

When God addresses them, their answers reveal how quickly fear becomes blame. The man points toward the woman and toward God who gave her to him. The woman points toward the serpent. Each describes something that happened, but neither begins with a plain acknowledgment of responsibility.

We recognize that pattern. We explain our impatience by another person's behavior. We defend our dishonesty by the pressure we faced. Context matters, but explanations can become places to hide.

God's encounter with the humans brings their actions into the open. Consequences follow. Yet the account also tells us that God provides clothing for them. Judgment does not erase His care.

John's account of the Word becoming flesh will bring us to the fullness of grace and truth in Jesus. We need both: truth that exposes what is wrong and grace that allows us to approach God honestly.

Repentance begins when we stop arranging the story to protect ourselves and tell the truth about our part in it.

**Practice:** Bring one specific failure before God without adding “but.” If someone was harmed, consider an honest apology and an appropriate step toward repair.

**Pray:** Merciful God, give me courage to tell the truth. Show me where I am hiding, forgive my sin, and help me turn toward faithful obedience. Amen.

## Day 5 — Answer for Your Brother

**Read:** Genesis 4:1–12; 6:5–8  
**Consider:** What is resentment asking me to do, and what faithful response could I choose instead?

Cain's story shows how disappointment can become resentment and resentment can become violence. Before the killing, God addresses Cain's anger and warns him about sin. Cain is called to respond before his anger determines his actions.

After Abel's death, Cain evades God's question about his brother. The account will not let us treat another person's life as irrelevant. Abel's blood cries out from the ground. Harm matters to God even when the person responsible would rather move on.

By Genesis 6, human wickedness has spread. The text speaks of God's grief over it. Our choices have consequences beyond ourselves, and God is attentive to the suffering they cause. The reading closes by introducing Noah as one who finds favor with the Lord.

This is a sober day in our study. It asks us to examine what we are cultivating. Anger can alert us to injustice, but it can also become permission to injure, belittle, or dismiss someone.

We cannot repair every wrong around us. We can take responsibility for the next action within our reach.

**Practice:** Notice one recurring resentment. Before responding to that person, pause. Choose a concrete action that resists harm: a measured reply, an appropriate boundary, an offer of help, or a refusal to spread gossip.

**Pray:** Lord, interrupt the habits that lead me to harm others. Help me take responsibility for my conduct and protect the dignity of my neighbors. Amen.

## Day 6 — Called by Name

**Read:** Isaiah 42:5–9; 43:1–10  
**Consider:** How can I receive God's comfort while becoming more attentive to others?

Isaiah identifies the Lord as the Creator who gives breath to the people of the earth. The God we encountered in Genesis is still speaking. His purposes include light, justice, and release for those in darkness.

In Isaiah 43, God addresses Jacob and Israel as a people He has created, formed, and redeemed. They are called by name and claimed as His own. The promise speaks of His presence through waters and fire. It does not describe a life free of danger.

We should first hear these words as words addressed to Israel. Their covenant setting matters. As followers of Jesus, we can learn from this testimony to God's faithfulness without erasing the people to whom He spoke.

John brings us to the Messiah who enters the world as its light. Trusting Him invites us to depend on God's faithfulness and to become attentive to people living in fear or isolation.

Comfort can move us outward. Remembering that God knows His people can prompt us to learn someone's name, listen to their story, and remain present through a difficult season.

**Practice:** Contact someone who may feel forgotten. Offer a specific, manageable form of support and follow through.

**Pray:** Faithful God, thank You for Your care for Israel and Your light revealed in Jesus. Help me trust You through difficulty and extend patient care to others. Amen.

## Day 7 — Welcome the Word

**Read:** John 1:1–14  
**Consider:** What would receiving Jesus change in my next ordinary decision?

The week began with God speaking light into creation. It ends with the Word entering the world He made.

John holds wonder and sorrow together. The Creator's Word comes into the world, yet the world does not recognize Him. His own do not receive Him. Nevertheless, those who receive Him and believe in His name are given the right to become children of God.

Receiving Jesus involves more than admiring an idea. We entrust ourselves to Him. We allow His truth to question our habits and His grace to draw us toward God.

The Word becomes flesh and dwells among us. God's self-revelation meets us within human life. Christian faith therefore reaches the places where we actually live: meals, work, rest, relationships, temptations, and responsibilities.

In Hebrew, Jesus is called Yeshua. Learning His Hebrew name can remind us of His Jewish setting; knowing Him calls us into faithful discipleship. This week, we have seen the goodness of creation and the seriousness of human sin. In Him, we encounter the Creator's light entering that wounded world.

End the week with a response you can carry into the next one.

**Practice:** Choose one practice from this week to continue. Tell a trusted person what you intend to do and ask them to check in with you.

**Pray:** Jesus, Messiah and Lord, help me receive You with trust. Shape my choices with Your grace and truth, and teach me to walk in Your light. Amen.

## Weekly closing — From the Beginning Toward Renewal

**Read:** Revelation 21:1–5

The Bible's closing vision gives us a new heaven and a new earth, God dwelling with His people, and an end to death and mourning. Creation's story leads us toward renewal in God's presence.

This hope does not make today's suffering insignificant. It gives us a future toward which we can live faithfully. Care for creation, honor another person, confess a wrong, keep a promise, and make room for rest. Such actions do not accomplish God's final renewal; they express trust in the God who promises it.

**For the home group:**

1. Which passage changed how you understood God or yourself this week?
2. Where did you recognize hiding, blame, or resentment in your own conduct?
3. What did you learn by hearing Isaiah's promises in their original setting?
4. Which daily practice will you carry forward, and how can the group support you?

**Closing prayer:** Creator and Redeemer, thank You for the goodness of Your creation and the hope of renewal. Form us into people who receive Your light and share Your care. Help us study honestly, repent readily, and live faithfully together. Amen.
